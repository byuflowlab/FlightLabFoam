FlightLabFoam

    Load an airplane from FlightLab

    Choose a .flightlab.json project saved from the FlightLab workbench, then pick the lifting surface to cut. The surface's stations become panels; airfoils, chords, sweep and twist carry over. An elevator hinge is loaded with cutting switched off. Cutting settings are kept.

      Sections run from the wing root out to the tip. Each panel spans two neighbouring sections. Sweep is the leading-edge offset, positive aft. Twist is about the leading edge, positive nose up.

      Spars

      Cuts on this page enter from the bottom of the wing unless you choose the top, so the top surface stays clean. Square and U spars are open slots cut in from that surface. Round spars are tubes, reached through a thin entry slit. Positions are fractions of the local chord, so a spar follows the taper; sizes are the same at both ends.

      Lightening holes

      A hot wire cannot start inside the foam, so each hole is reached through a thin slit, from the bottom unless you choose the top.

      Control surfaces

      Cuts through the wing along the hinge line and back, separating the aileron or elevator. A gap makes the cut a V that opens toward the entry surface, leaving room to deflect. A cut runs the full length of each panel it applies to.

      Block placement

      Where the block sits in the cutting area. Program zero is where you zero the wire before running the file. With both distances from program zero at 0, program zero is the bottom edge of the block's entry face, the simplest setup. To zero the wire at a fixed point on the machine instead, such as a corner of the table, set the block down a measured distance from it and enter that distance here.

      Panel placement

      Where the panel sits inside the block. The cut-in is the foam the wire crosses before it reaches the airfoil. The offsets move the panel from the centred position.

      Cutting settings

      G-code is always written in millimetres. The feed basis says where the cutting speed is held: on the wing root or tip, or on the faster or slower carriage. On a tapered panel the other end moves slower, so it melts more; give it more kerf.

      The airfoil as designed (dashed), the outline after the minimum thicknesses (shaded), and the path the wire follows, offset outside it by the kerf. Spars, holes and control cuts show on the path. The vertical line marks where the trailing-edge thickness starts.

      The orange line is the wire between the two carriages. The foam block and the panel are drawn between them. The time shown assumes the machine moves exactly at the programmed feeds.
